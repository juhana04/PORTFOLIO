Case study

Legal-Ease

Designing a tool that helps people understand Terms and Conditions before they click “Agree”.

Type3rd-year mini project
My roleUI/UX design and development
ToolsFigma, FastAPI, TailwindCSS
FocusInformed digital consent

The problem

Most people accept Terms and Conditions without reading them. The documents are long and full of legal language, so risky clauses about data sharing or automatic renewal go unnoticed.

The goal

Give users a clear answer to one question: is this document safe for me to accept? The design had to work in seconds, without requiring any legal knowledge.

User flow

1Paste or upload the document
2Read the plain-language summary
3Check the Risk Meter
4Open flagged clauses
5Decide: accept or review

Design decisions

  • Risk Meter first. A single gauge gives an at-a-glance verdict before the user reads any detail.
  • Summary in plain language. It replaces pages of legal text with a short explanation, which reduces reading effort.
  • Flagged clauses as a short list. Each clause shows a severity label as text, not just color, so the meaning is clear to everyone, including people who can’t distinguish the colors.
Dashboard concept: summary, Risk Meter and flagged clauses

Usability test plan

The plan is to test the prototype with 3 to 5 people who are not designers. Each person completes the same tasks while thinking aloud, and I record task completion, time taken, errors and a 1 to 5 ease rating.

TaskSuccess looks likeResult
1. Find the summary of a pasted documentFinds it within 30 seconds, no helpTo be recorded
2. Explain the Risk Meter in your own wordsCorrectly says whether the document is riskyTo be recorded
3. Find the clause about sharing dataOpens the flagged clause unaidedTo be recorded
4. Decide whether to accept, and say whyGives a reason based on the findingsTo be recorded
Findings and design changes will be added here after the test sessions.

What I’d do next

Run the sessions, fix the biggest problems first, then retest. I also want to check the colors and text sizes against WCAG contrast guidelines.
